## safety_001: Collision Repair Safety and System Restoration
* **Reference ID**: safety_001
* **Classification**: Public Government Safety Reference (Informational Technical Guidance -- Not an Insurance Policy or Decision Rule)
* **Topic**: Collision repair safety and system restoration
* **Source Organization**: National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA)
* **Source Document**: NHTSA Interpretation Letter, 49 U.S.C. 30122 (Make Inoperative Provision)
* **Source URL**: https://www.nhtsa.gov/interpretations/30122-make-inoperative-alan-nappier-april-14
* **Technical Guidance**: Safety devices and crash protection systems installed in compliance with Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards must be fully restored to vehicle manufacturer operating specifications following collision damage. When repairing crash-damaged vehicles, repair facilities must ensure that all active and passive occupant protection components, structural elements, and safety-critical devices remain fully operational to provide continuous occupant protection over the vehicle service life. Installed safety features and design elements must not be rendered inoperative or left compromised during repair operations.

## safety_002: Post-Collision Safety-System Considerations
* **Reference ID**: safety_002
* **Classification**: Public Government Safety Reference (Informational Technical Guidance -- Not an Insurance Policy or Decision Rule)
* **Topic**: Post-collision safety-system inspection and restoration
* **Source Organization**: National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA)
* **Source Document**: NHTSA Interpretation Letter, 2256y (Airbag Replacement Guidance)
* **Source URL**: https://www.nhtsa.gov/interpretations/2256y
* **Technical Guidance**: Post-collision inspection, servicing, and restoration are required for all vehicle safety systems that may have sustained impact forces or damage, including seat belt assemblies, pretensioners, braking components, and supplemental restraint systems (SRS). Deployed air bags must be replaced with specified replacement units according to vehicle manufacturer technical specifications. Because restraint system restoration procedures are not federally standardized across vehicle manufacturers, technicians must follow OEM-specific repair procedures and inspection protocols for the exact vehicle make, model, and model year.

## safety_003: ADAS Inspection and Calibration Considerations
* **Reference ID**: safety_003
* **Classification**: Public Industry Technical Reference (Informational Technical Guidance -- Not an Insurance Policy or Decision Rule)
* **Topic**: ADAS inspection, sensor alignment, and calibration
* **Source Organization**: Inter-Industry Conference on Auto Collision Repair (I-CAR)
* **Source Document**: I-CAR Repairability Technical Support (RTS) Portal, CRN-689 (Typical Calibration Requirements)
* **Source URL**: https://rts.i-car.com/crn-689.html
* **Technical Guidance**: Vehicles equipped with Advanced Driver Assistance Systems (ADAS) require sensor calibration whenever exterior panels, mounting structures, or sensor housings are disturbed during collision repair. Calibration is required after operations such as bumper cover removal, windshield replacement, side-mirror servicing, suspension alignment, or removal of any component housing camera, radar, or ultrasonic sensors, because physical mounting alignment relative to vehicle thrust line can shift even in the absence of direct sensor impact. Electronic diagnostic fault code scanning and physical ADAS calibration are distinct operations: diagnostic scanning detects electronic diagnostic trouble codes (DTCs), while calibration establishes correct spatial positioning and aim. Technicians must adhere to vehicle-specific OEM calibration instructions, target placement specifications, and environment conditions for each vehicle model and installed option package.

## safety_004: EV and High-Voltage Collision Safety
* **Reference ID**: safety_004
* **Classification**: Public Industry Technical Reference (Informational Technical Guidance -- Not an Insurance Policy or Decision Rule)
* **Topic**: Electric vehicle high-voltage collision safety and de-energization
* **Source Organization**: Industry EV Collision-Safety Guidance (Midtronics and I-CAR Shop Practices)
* **Source Document**: Midtronics and I-CAR Technical Service Guidance (Disconnecting High-Voltage EV Systems)
* **Source URL**: https://www.midtronics.com/blog/disconnect-high-voltage-ev-battery-systems/
* **Technical Guidance**: Modern electric and hybrid vehicles operate high-voltage electrical architectures typically between 400V and 800V. Automatic pyrofuses or contactors isolate the high-voltage pack upon major impact, but damaged battery enclosures or internal cells can experience delayed thermal runaway hours or days after an impact. Technicians must perform high-voltage safety isolation before performing physical repairs or inspections: disconnect the 12V auxiliary system, remove the manual service disconnect (MSD) or high-voltage service plug, observe manufacturer discharge waiting intervals (typically 5 to 15 minutes) for intermediate capacitors to discharge, and verify zero voltage using a properly rated CAT III or CAT IV 1000V multimeter. Technicians must not rely on dashboard indicators to confirm de-energization. Damaged high-voltage batteries require physical isolation and continuous temperature monitoring following OEM and facility safety guidelines. There is no industry mandate requiring automatic replacement of undamaged high-voltage battery assemblies without OEM diagnostic confirmation.

## safety_005: General Repair Documentation and OEM-Procedure Considerations
* **Reference ID**: safety_005
* **Classification**: Public Government Safety Reference (Informational Technical Guidance -- Not an Insurance Policy or Decision Rule)
* **Topic**: General repair documentation, component standards, and OEM procedures
* **Source Organization**: National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA)
* **Source Document**: NHTSA Interpretation Letter, 21331ogm (Replacement Parts Crashworthiness)
* **Source URL**: https://www.nhtsa.gov/node/51671
* **Technical Guidance**: Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards (FMVSS) regulate new vehicles and specific replacement equipment components, including brake hoses, exterior lamps, tires, automotive safety glass, and seat belt assemblies. Federal safety standards do not establish separate replacement crashworthiness standards for exterior body panels or structural sheet metal. Because vehicle structural design, advanced high-strength steel (AHSS) joining methods, and passive restraint integration differ significantly across vehicle platforms, collision repair facilities must follow vehicle-specific OEM repair procedures for the exact make, model, and model year rather than relying on generalized assumptions.
